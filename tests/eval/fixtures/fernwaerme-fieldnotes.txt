Unreleased field notes from the Fernwaerme district heating controller rollout.
Collected by the on-site technicians during the pilot winter. No structure yet,
these are raw diary entries that will be edited into proper release notes later.

The calibration routine for the flow sensor drifts whenever the district water
temperature drops below forty degrees. We measured a drift of up to two
percent per week on sensor bank A. The fix so far is a nightly recalibration
job that writes offset tables into the controller EEPROM. Technicians should
not hand-edit those offset tables because the nightly job overwrites manual
changes without warning.

Sensor bank B uses a different die and barely drifts at all, maybe a tenth of
a percent over the same week. The purchase order for bank B cited the tighter
datasheet tolerance. We now route the nightly recalibration only at bank A
controllers and skip bank B entirely, which cut the EEPROM write cycles by
about half. Write endurance on the bank A EEPROM is rated for one hundred
thousand cycles and we were burning through roughly forty cycles a night
before the split.

The valve maintenance schedule follows the manufacturer table for the Vorex
2500 series. Grease intervals are every six months for actuator shafts and
every two years for the gearbox. Districts with hard water should halve the
gearbox interval because mineral deposits stiffen the packing gland. We keep
per-district interval overrides in the maintenance ledger, keyed by the
district short code.

During the cold snap in February two Vorex actuators seized after the packing
gland dried out. The emergency procedure is to open the bypass loop first,
then cut the actuator power, then unbolt the gland cover. Reversing those
steps once pressurised the bonnet and blew a gasket across the plant room.
Nobody was hurt but the gasket inventory rule now requires two spares per
district on site at all times.

The firmware update procedure for the controller line starts with a memory
dump from the running image. The dump goes onto a USB stick formatted with
FAT32, anything else is invisible to the bootloader. The bootloader then
verifies the image signature against the factory key burned into the secure
element. Unsigned images drop the controller into a rescue shell that only
speaks serial at 115200 baud.

Rescue shell access needs a null modem cable and a terminal that renders
seven data bits, even parity. The factory terminal profile for that is stored
in the toolkit as profile F7E. Technicians keep bricking the serial settings
because the controller ignores eight data bit profiles silently. The rescue
shell can mount the USB stick, reflash the image, and reboot, which takes
about eleven minutes end to end.

Error code 4100 means the primary loop pump lost prime. Error code 4101 means
the secondary loop pump lost prime. Error code 4200 means the expansion
vessel precharge is below the datasheet floor. Error code 5100 means the
flue gas temperature sensor reads open circuit. These four codes cover most
of the winter callouts and the on-call rota keeps a laminated card with the
pump priming steps clipped next to it.

The callout rota itself is week based, Monday to Sunday, with a handover
meeting at eight on Monday mornings. Handover notes go into the shared ledger
under the outgoing week number. If a callout happens during handover the
outgoing technician keeps the ticket. This rule exists because two winters
ago a ticket sat unassigned for a day while both technicians assumed the
other one had taken it, and a school building went a full day without heat.
